# Scientific scope and limitations

The work studies **model-conditional identifiability**, not a universal operational procedure for determining a particle's spin.

**Established in the specified model:** internal rank-2 absence for spin 1/2; primitive-resource criterion within the chosen response architecture and nuisance class; all-finite-bandwidth magnetic-only theorem for a nonblind moment domain; 63 sufficient separation certificates and 33 constructed overlaps out of 99 broad-class cells with *unfiltered* $A_C=1$; moment-relation robustness and estimator-space numerical demonstrations.

**Not established:** a complete energy–momentum-conserving detector scattering likelihood, an experimentally demonstrated apparatus, a particle discovery, a universal theorem for arbitrary form-factor classes or acceptances, or an answer for the three unresolved points $(0.08,24)$, $(0.10,16)$, $(0.50,1)$.

**Numerical evidence:** positive envelope evaluations and signed witness endpoints are numerical, not formally validated interval proofs. Finite-basis QCQP positivity does not establish separation over the entire Lipschitz function class. SOS certificates are floating-point numerical feasibility checks.

**Acceptances:** the primary charge model has $A_C(k)=1$; the family $A_C^{(\alpha)}(k)=[2m/(E_k+m)]^\alpha$ provides **optional fixed-acceptance sensitivity cases** ($\alpha=1,2,3$), not a preferred physics-derived selection. The four fixed $\alpha$ cases do not establish a theorem uniform over continuous $\alpha$.

Auxiliary collider and generator scripts are available as separate provenance, but do not validate the detector model used for the present theoretical claims.
