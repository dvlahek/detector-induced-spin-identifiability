# Scientific scope and limitations

This research examines **model-conditional identifiability** of charged spin-1/2 versus spin-1 candidates from specified positive, calibrated electromagnetic detector-response blocks.

## Established within the model

1. Spin 1/2 lacks an internal tensor rank-2 sector; spin 1 can support it.
2. Architecture-aware resource support depends on the admitted nuisance class and which physical detector blocks are available.
3. A nonblind magnetic anchor domain yields a positive magnetic-only margin for every **finite** Gaussian bandwidth and finite Lipschitz bound.
4. For the specified broad $\delta=0$ reference class, sufficient whole-class certificates and constructed origin overlaps settle 97 of 99 tested grid cells; two remain unresolved.
5. Uniform $|\delta|\leq\Delta$ bounds and four fixed positive charge-acceptance choices quantify selected model sensitivities.

## Not established or claimed

- A fully energy–momentum conserving scattering apparatus, likelihood model, operational detector or observed particle discovery.
- A universal spin-identifiability statement across all theories, all moment domains or all detector acceptances.
- A complete event-level reproduction of the auxiliary collider study; the modified vector UFO and the reconstructed event sample are absent.
- A whole-class answer for either unresolved cell; a positive finite-basis QCQP optimum is not such a proof.
- Formal interval certification of every decimal or numerically checked witness sign. Analytic inequalities and IVT existence statements are kept distinct from their numerical inputs.
- Uniform-in-$\alpha$ robustness inferred from only the four evaluated acceptance exponents.

## Selected charge acceptance

The reference model specifies a nonnegative momentum-dependent efficiency:

$$
A_C(k)=\left(\frac{2m}{E_k+m}\right)^2.
$$

This is a **choice of detector architecture**, not a universal implication of the Ward–Takahashi identity. A different acceptance changes the finite-bandwidth charge measure and may change sufficient certificates; the magnetic theorem is independent of it.

They should not be read as experimental evidence for the claims of the present manuscript.
